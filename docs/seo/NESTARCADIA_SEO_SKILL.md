## 3. Non-negotiable operating principles

1. **Evidence before edits.** Inspect the live URL, current repository, current deployment, and relevant reports before changing anything.
2. **People first.** Create pages for real users and genuine service intent. Search terms inform the page; they do not dictate awkward copy.
3. **Truth over optimisation.** Never invent business facts, addresses, service coverage, projects, testimonials, awards, prices, review counts, schema properties, or performance results.
4. **No guaranteed outcomes.** Technical compliance, schema validity, indexing requests, or good PageSpeed metrics do not guarantee indexing, rankings, rich results, or traffic.
5. **Small, reversible changes.** Change the smallest relevant surface. Preserve design, conversions, accessibility, analytics, consent, and existing functionality.
6. **Verify what shipped.** A Git commit or successful build is not proof that Hostinger serves the new result. Confirm deployment and inspect the live output.
7. **Separate fact from hypothesis.** Label measured findings, inferred causes, and unverified assumptions distinctly.
8. **Current source hierarchy.** For Google Search behaviour, prefer current Google Search Central / Google Business Profile documentation. For schema vocabulary, use Schema.org. For site state, prefer live HTTP/source inspection, repository code and Search Console evidence. Do not rely on old blog posts when official docs exist.
9. **Direct output.** Report the issue, evidence, risk, action, verification and remaining blocker. Avoid generic advice and filler.

## 5. Local SEO strategy for NestArcadia

### Primary market architecture

Build a clear hierarchy:
- Homepage: overall business and primary coverage of Noida Extension and Greater Noida West.
- Primary local service pages: one page for Noida Extension and one for Greater Noida West, each with unique, genuinely useful local information.
- Service pages: residential interiors, 2BHK/3BHK, villas, turnkey execution, commercial/office interiors, custom storage/furniture, only where the service is actually offered.
- Journal/articles: material guides, layout/space planning, execution explainers, budget considerations, practical design advice, and project case studies.

### Local page quality gate

Do not create a page for every society, locality, or keyword variation by swapping place names. A location page must add unique local value, for example:
- Verified project or site experience in that locality.
- Relevant apartment/villa constraints or actual layout considerations.
- Genuine local material, logistics, execution or consultation details.
- Original photographs or case studies with permission and accurate captions.
- Relevant FAQs based on real customer questions.
- Clear next step to enquire or share a floor plan.

If there is no meaningful unique value, consolidate the content into the appropriate primary area page rather than generating a thin doorway page.

### Google Business Profile

Keep business name, phone, address/service area, categories, hours, website, photos and services accurate and consistent with real-world operations. Do not keyword-stuff the business name. Do not create fake locations or reviews. Never promise a local ranking. Explain that local visibility is influenced mainly by relevance, distance and prominence; distance cannot be manipulated through website copy.

### Society and neighbourhood references

Use society names (e.g., ATS Greens, Cleo County, Mahagun Moderne, Gaur City, Ace Parkway) only when relevant and factually grounded. Do not imply completed work, client relationships, endorsements, or local presence unless confirmed. Avoid repetitive lists of society names purely for keywords.

## 6. Keyword research and page mapping

1. Start with the user's service, property type, geography, and conversion intent.
2. Review Search Console queries/pages where access is available; use impressions, clicks, CTR and average position as observed data, not guarantees.
3. Research current search results and competitors when the task asks for strategy or current opportunities. Prefer primary and trustworthy sources; distinguish paid results from organic.
4. Build one primary intent per page with closely related secondary intents. Avoid cannibalising multiple pages for the same query.
5. Prioritise qualified commercial intent (e.g., interior designers in Noida Extension, 2BHK interior design, 3BHK interiors, turnkey interior execution) and useful informational support content.
6. Do not force exact-match keywords into every heading, paragraph, alt attribute or anchor.
7. A keyword map must identify target page, primary intent, secondary themes, internal links, proof/evidence required and conversion action.

## 7. On-page content standards

For every important page, inspect and improve as needed:
- A unique, descriptive, concise `<title>` aligned to the visible page content.
- A useful meta description that accurately summarises the page; treat it as a snippet suggestion, not a guaranteed Google snippet.
- One clear main heading and a logical heading hierarchy.
- Introductory copy that immediately explains the service, audience and geography where relevant.
- Original content that demonstrates actual design/execution knowledge.
- Contextual internal links with descriptive anchor text.
- Clear business identity and contact routes.
- Accessible, descriptive image alt text when the image conveys information; decorative imagery should have empty alt text.
- Accurate Open Graph/social metadata where required.
- Canonical URL matching the intended indexable URL.

Never promise exact title length, meta-description length, keyword density, or a fixed number of headings as a Google ranking rule. Keep copy readable and concise; Google may rewrite titles and snippets.

## 8. Content quality and spam prevention

Every proposed article or landing page must pass these tests:
- Who is the intended reader?
- What real question or decision does the page help with?
- What original evidence, experience, analysis, photographs, diagrams, examples or expert input does NestArcadia add?
- Is the information accurate and current?
- Is the page distinct enough to deserve its own URL?
- Does it help a potential customer move toward a sensible next step?

Do not produce:
- Large batches of near-identical AI location pages.
- Keyword-stuffed text, hidden text, cloaking or misleading redirects.
- Scraped or lightly rewritten competitor articles.
- Fake reviews, fake authors, fabricated case studies or fabricated results.
- Paid links or link exchanges intended to manipulate ranking signals.
- Doorway pages, expired-domain abuse, parasite/site-reputation abuse, or scaled content with little original value.

AI may support research, drafting, outlining and editing. Human verification and meaningful original value are mandatory; automation volume is not a quality strategy.

## 9. Internal linking and site architecture

- Ensure key service pages are reachable through normal crawlable `<a href>` links.
- Link from relevant Journal articles to the exact relevant service/locality page, and link back to useful supporting articles where appropriate.
- Use concise, descriptive anchor text; avoid generic repeated anchors and over-optimised exact-match patterns.
- Keep navigation logical and shallow enough for users to find core services.
- Avoid orphan pages. Confirm important pages are present in XML sitemap and linked internally.
- Use canonical URL conventions consistently (host, HTTPS, trailing slash policy, case, query parameters).

## 10. Technical SEO workflow

Inspect before changing:
1. HTTP response and redirect chain for apex and `www` domain.
2. `robots.txt`, XML sitemap, canonical tags, meta robots and X-Robots-Tag.
3. Raw HTML and rendered HTML for page title, description, H1, body content, internal links and JSON-LD.
4. HTTP status of all intended landing pages; distinguish real pages from SPA fallback pages returning 200.
5. Canonical target, internal link target and sitemap URL agreement.
6. JavaScript errors, missing assets, blocked resources and console errors.
7. Duplicate titles, meta descriptions, canonicals, JSON-LD nodes and conflicting schema entities.
8. Mobile and desktop rendering, forms, WhatsApp CTA, navigation and accessibility.

For JavaScript/SPA sites, do not assume Google will immediately render all app content. Prefer server/static pre-rendering for critical page content when safe. Keep initial HTML useful, unique per route, and aligned with rendered content. Do not use JavaScript to change canonical to a different URL than the one in the original HTML. Prevent duplicate canonical tags.

## 11. Structured data / Schema.org guardrails

### General

- Use JSON-LD unless there is a reason not to.
- Choose a type and properties that accurately describe the real entity/page.
- A Schema.org property being valid does not automatically mean Google supports a rich result for it.
- Follow BOTH Schema.org vocabulary rules and Google's structured data feature policies.
- Mark up only visible, accurate, current content. Do not add fake reviews, ratings, offers, prices, opening hours, address, service claims, or locations.
- Use stable, absolute `@id` URLs and connect entities consistently (`WebSite`, `Organization`/business entity, `WebPage`, `Service`, `BreadcrumbList`, `Article` where appropriate).
- Keep static and runtime JSON-LD coordinated: one intended graph per page, stable script handling, no duplicate entity nodes with conflicting details.
- Validate JSON syntax, Schema.org vocabulary, Google's Rich Results Test where applicable, and rendered page source.
- A valid result does not guarantee a rich result or ranking boost.

### NestArcadia-specific schema

- Use a business type that truthfully describes the real operation; verify exact subtype/property compatibility against Schema.org before adding fields.
- Do not put `serviceType` on `ProfessionalService` when the validator reports it unsupported for that type. Represent offered services through suitable `Service` nodes and/or `hasOfferCatalog` with `Offer` → `itemOffered` → `Service`, after checking current vocabulary.
- `serviceType` may be appropriate on a `Service` node; validate the exact expected type before use.
- `WebSite.publisher` should reference the canonical business/organization `@id`.
- Do not fabricate a street address just to clear an optional warning. If the business is genuinely a service-area business with no customer-facing location, model according to current Google/Schema.org guidance and the actual Business Profile configuration.
- Use `areaServed` only for areas genuinely served; do not add every NCR locality just to widen keyword coverage.
- `priceRange`, address, hours and sameAs values must reflect current public facts.
- Do not expect FAQ schema to guarantee FAQ rich results. Use FAQs primarily to answer real user questions.

## 12. Image and video SEO

### Images
- Prefer responsive, compressed formats (e.g., WebP/AVIF where supported) with a reliable fallback.
- Use meaningful filenames when practical, correct image dimensions, `srcset`/`sizes` where appropriate, and correct MIME type.
- Do not lazy-load the real above-the-fold LCP hero; prioritise the correct responsive hero resource. Lazy-load below-the-fold non-critical images.
- Use truthful alt text describing the actual image. Add locality/style only if the visual/context really supports it. Do not repeat target keywords mechanically.
- Ensure important images are discoverable in rendered HTML and not hidden behind user interaction.

### Videos
- Provide a crawlable page containing a visible video and useful surrounding content when video search visibility is a goal.
- Use a stable, fetchable thumbnail and accurate video title/description.
- Add `VideoObject` only when its required values accurately describe the video and comply with Google's current video structured data guidance.
- Do not add video schema to a page where the video is not a meaningful visible element.
- For YouTube embeds, ensure the watch page offers distinct user value beyond merely embedding a player.

## 13. Performance and Core Web Vitals

Always distinguish real-user field data from Lighthouse/PageSpeed lab data. Never treat one lab run or a PageSpeed score as the whole performance diagnosis.

Good Core Web Vitals thresholds at the 75th percentile:
- LCP: 2.5 seconds or less.
- INP: 200 milliseconds or less.
- CLS: 0.1 or less.

Use CrUX/Search Console field data to understand real user experience where available. Use Lighthouse/PageSpeed lab runs to diagnose. Compare the same URL, device form factor and test configuration; rerun to account for variability.

Diagnosis order:
1. Confirm the tested canonical URL and redirect chain.
2. Identify the LCP element and request chain; check image URL, size, format, preload/fetch priority, server response and redirects.
3. Inspect render-blocking CSS/fonts, font loading, critical CSS and layout shifts.
4. Inspect JS bundle sizes, main-thread long tasks, hydration/render work and unused JS.
5. Audit third-party tags (GTM, GA4, Ads, chat/widgets/maps) and their loading/consent behaviour.
6. Check caching, compression, CDN/server TTFB and asset MIME/cache headers.
7. Validate desktop and mobile separately. Never degrade mobile to improve desktop or vice versa.

For each proposed performance edit:
- Capture a baseline.
- Make one grouped but targeted change.
- Run production build and browser tests.
- Confirm hero/currentSrc, all critical requests, page errors, LCP/INP/CLS where measurable, forms and tracking.
- Repeat PageSpeed on the same canonical URL and compare.
- Roll back if there is a regression or tracking/functionality break.

## 14. Measurement and reporting

Use the most relevant evidence source:
- Google Search Console: indexing, URL inspection, sitemap status, manual actions, query/page performance, page experience and CWV field data.
- GA4: qualified enquiries, lead events, landing pages, channel attribution and conversion paths.
- PageSpeed Insights/Lighthouse: lab diagnostics and field data when supplied.

- Live browser/network tests: actual current source, response codes, MIME types, redirects, console and request errors.
- Google Business Profile: real profile completeness, service area, categories, review/reputation signals and customer actions.

Report:
- Baseline and date.
- URL/device and field-vs-lab classification.
- Measured values and exact failing opportunities.
- Confirmed root cause vs hypothesis.
- Files/settings changed.
- Build/test/deployment evidence.
- Remaining issues and next highest-impact action.

Never say "fixed" until the change is live and a relevant verification passes. If only code or source changed, say so explicitly.
